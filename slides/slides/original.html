The original model: RG-ResMoE

1. Stock features x (16)
raw 5/20/60-day volatility, returns, RSI

2. Regime variables z (2)
raw market and stock-specific volatility

3. Base network
trained first, then frozen

4. Four correction experts
small networks, start at zero

5. Regime gate
sees x and z, picks expert weights

6. Weighted correction
Σ weight × expert

7. Forecast: next-5-day volatility
base forecast + weighted correction

The weak spot: volatility goes in on a raw scale. Fine in normal markets; a crash lands far outside the training range.

expert weights

Ye & Borde, “Regime-Gated Residual Mixture-of-Experts for Cross-Sectional Volatility Forecasting”, arXiv:2608.12251, ICAIF ’26. Diagram redrawn from the paper.

This is the published architecture. Stock features go to a frozen base network and four small correction experts. Only the gate sees the regime variables: market volatility and stock-specific volatility. It decides how much each expert’s correction counts. The paper’s point is that regime information should route corrections, not be an extra input. The weak spot, in orange, is that volatility goes in on a raw scale.
